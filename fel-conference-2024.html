28th Annual Conference · FEL XXVIII

Endangered Languages and Oral Traditions

The Foundation for Endangered Languages (FEL) and the Forum for Language Initiatives (FLI), in collaboration with Allama Iqbal Open University Islamabad, held the 28th Annual Conference of FEL in Islamabad, Pakistan.

The Theme

Endangered Languages and Oral Traditions

Oral traditions and expressions are very significant aspects of intangible linguistic and cultural heritage. They are the collective memory of a community and play a crucial role in keeping languages and cultures alive. They come in a huge variety of spoken forms: folk songs, stories, riddles, tales, legends, myths, poems, epics, chants, charms, romances and other types of folk literature and dramatic performances.

Languages live and prosper in oral traditions. When languages are threatened and become extinct, oral traditions and expressions are also permanently lost. Documenting and safeguarding oral traditions are therefore significant ways of protecting and preserving languages — important complements to writing dictionaries and creating grammars and databases.

FEL XXVIII provided a forum for examining the relationship between endangered languages and oral traditions, with a universal scope and a particular focus on the oral traditions of the indigenous communities of Pakistan.

Sub-themes

  • Endangered oral literatures: heritage preservation (music, poetry, mushaira, contests…)
  • Oral cultures and traditional knowledge
  • Documentation and digitalization of oral art and literature
  • Language policy, planning, and oral art
  • Oral art and mother tongue education
  • Mother-tongue education policies: oral art and literature
  • Rediscovering oral traditions and expressions
  • Oral traditions as a vehicle for transmission of culture and language

Practical information for participants

Abstract submission (closed)

Abstracts of 600–800 words were invited on EasyChair by 31 May 2024. Authors of accepted abstracts submitted a conference paper of 2,000–3,000 words by 15 August 2024. Conference papers were compiled as a conference book for participants and FEL members.

Format and excursion

The conference was held in hybrid format and streamed online for registered participants. On the last day, a one-day excursion was organised to an indigenous community in the Hazara Division of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa for those attending in person.

Visa guide for international participants

International participants applied for a Tourist / Visit Visa online through the Pakistan e-Visa portal (visa.nadra.gov.pk), receiving electronic authorisation to travel.

  • Required: completed application form, passport valid for six months, photograph, conference invitation letter, letter of recommendation, proof of accommodation, travel itinerary and proof of payment
  • Apply at least 6 weeks in advance; online applications take 7–10 business days, in-person 7–15
Getting from Islamabad International Airport to the venue

Taxi: about 30–45 minutes, PKR 1,500–2,500 (USD 9–15). Ride-hailing apps such as Careem, inDrive or Yango are recommended.

Airport shuttle and Metro Bus: about 1.5–2 hours, around PKR 300. Take the shuttle to the Metro Bus terminal, ride to Faiz Ahmad Faiz station, then a taxi to AIOU.

Islamabad in September

September marks the transition from the monsoon to autumn: daytime temperatures of 25–30°C and nights of 15–20°C. Popular sites include Faisal Mosque, the Pakistan Monument, Daman-e-Koh, Lok Virsa Museum, Rawal Lake, Saidpur Village and Pir Sohawa.

Preserving the wealth of regional cultures and languages of Pakistan

Established in 2002 to assist the minoritized language communities of northern Pakistan in preserving and developing their mother tongues. We put mother-tongue speakers in the driving seat of language development and cultural documentation.
