About Forum for Language Initiatives

Empowering minoritized ethnolinguistic communities of northern Pakistan to revitalize, document, and celebrate their mother tongues through community ownership, literacy, and policy advocacy.

Who We Are & Historical Roots

Preserving Northern Pakistan’s Living Heritage Since 2003

The Forum for Language Initiatives (FLI) is a pioneer non-governmental linguistic resource center established in 2003 and headquartered in Islamabad, Pakistan.

Pakistan is home to over 70 living languages. Yet more than 30 minoritized mountain idioms spoken across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (Chitral, Swat, Kohistan, Dir), Gilgit-Baltistan (Hunza, Gilgit, Baltistan), Azad Jammu & Kashmir, and South Waziristan are classified as endangered by UNESCO.

FLI was formed by indigenous scholars, community elders, and educators to reverse this trajectory. Rather than treating languages as passive academic specimens, FLI works hand-in-hand with native speakers to establish Community-Based Organizations (CBOs), standardize writing orthographies, develop multilingual pre-primary education (MTB-MLE), publish vernacular folk literature, and engineer digital Nastaliq fonts and mobile keyboards.

Our Vision

To See all Ethnolinguistic Communities Flourishing Culturally and Economically

We envision a pluralistic Pakistan where every linguistic community experiences total cultural dignity, social inclusion, and economic advancement through the continuous use and development of their ancestral mother tongue.

تمام نسلی و لسانی گروہوں کو اپنی زبان و ثقافت کو برقرار رکھتے ہوئے پروان چڑھتے اور ترقی کرتے دیکھنا۔

Our Mission

Empowering Minoritized Language Communities

To assist the minoritized language communities of Pakistan—especially those inhabiting the northern mountainous areas—in preserving and developing their mother tongues, cultural heritage, and literature through research, training, orthography development, and mother-tongue education.

پاکستان کی غیر غالب لسانی برادریوں کو اپنی مادری زبانوں کے تحفظ اور فروغ کے لیے بااختیار بنانا۔

Institutional Ethics & Culture

Our 6 Core Values

Guiding principles established by FLI’s governing charter:

1

Respect for Diversity

We honor each language and culture as an irreplaceable treasury of human knowledge, wisdom, and local biodiversity stewardship.

2

Community Ownership

We believe lasting language revitalization succeeds only when local mother-tongue speakers lead research, decisions, and curriculum design.

3

Grassroots Participation

Our programs involve village elders, schoolteachers, women storytellers, and youth equally in orthography development and cultural documentation.

4

Academic Rigor

We apply modern sociolinguistic standards, acoustic phonology, and ISO 639-3 classifications to every dictionary and grammar sketch.

5

Inclusivity & Equity

We advocate for the linguistic human rights of minoritized mountain communities without bias to ethnicity, geography, or socioeconomic status.

6

Sustainability & Digital Vitality

Bridging ancient oral heritage into the 21st century through Unicode keyboards, digital storytelling, and multilingual school curricula.

Strategic Roadmap

Our 5 Strategic Objectives

The five core mandates governing FLI’s operations and training programs:

1

Capacity Building of Language Communities

Train mother-tongue speakers in linguistic survey methods, descriptive phonology, orthography development, grammar formulation, lexicography, and creative literature production.

2

Promoting Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE)

Advocate for and establish early childhood mother-tongue schooling models, developing graded primers, big books, and pedagogical materials that transition children smoothly to Urdu and English.

3

Documentation & Vernacular Literature Production

Record, transcribe, and publish oral traditions, poetry, folk proverbs, ethnographic accounts, and dictionaries to preserve endangered intangible cultural heritage.

4

Digital Language Vitality & Keyboard Development

Ensure minoritized languages thrive in the digital sphere by engineering custom Android keyboards, Keyman packages for PC, and standardizing Nastaliq and Arabic unicode glyphs.

5

Linguistic Human Rights & Policy Advocacy

Engage parliamentarians, provincial assemblies (KP, GB, AJK), educational directorates, and international organizations (UNESCO) to secure legal recognition and curriculum inclusion.

Methodology

Our 4-Pillar Model of Language Revitalization

The cyclical framework developed by FLI to move languages from oral vulnerability to published sustainability:

Pillar 1

Survey & Sociolinguistics

Assessing language vitality, dialectal variation, bilingualism rates, and community attitudes to determine urgent documentation priorities.

Pillar 2

Orthography & Phonology

Organizing orthography conferences with village elders, scholars, and teachers to establish consensus on standard Arabic/Nastaliq alphabets.

Pillar 3

MTB-MLE Education

Producing early childhood curriculum, training local community teachers, and piloting community-run mother-tongue schools.

Pillar 4

Literature & Digital Vitality

Publishing folklore anthologies, dictionaries, mobile Android keyboards, and digital media to ensure the language thrives in daily modern life.
