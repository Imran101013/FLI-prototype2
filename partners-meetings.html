FLI Hosts Regional Partners’ Meeting in Peshawar

September 4, 2025 · Peshawar, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

The Forum for Language Initiatives (FLI) convened a partners’ meeting in Peshawar, bringing together leaders from 14 Community-Based Organizations (CBOs) across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. The one-day gathering served as a platform for reflection, collaboration and renewed commitment to indigenous language preservation.

Over the past six months, these CBOs have been actively engaged in grassroots efforts to strengthen their mother tongues. Following the presentations, FLI facilitated a session on promoting indigenous languages through poetry, and participants were encouraged to engage poets from their communities.

The meeting also explored strategies to expand language documentation across diverse domains. In a major development, a provincial-level regional network was formed to unify efforts across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, with a dedicated WhatsApp group for ongoing coordination.

Nasir Mansur — Kateviri

Praised for working with a government school in his village, where he trained children to deliver speeches in their native language during Independence Day celebrations on August 14.

Alauddin — Yadgha

Reported writers’ workshops held in both private and government schools in his region, nurturing literary expression among students.

Aurangzeb — Mankiyali

The first-time representation of Mankiyali, spoken by fewer than 500 people in Mansehra district — a significant step for the visibility of this endangered language.

Closing resolution

Participants committed to advocating for linguistic rights through their elected representatives. Regional delegations were announced to engage members of the provincial assembly, aiming to push forward a resolution reinforcing the government’s commitment to indigenous language support.

Preserving the wealth of regional cultures and languages of Pakistan

Established in 2002 to assist the minoritized language communities of northern Pakistan in preserving and developing their mother tongues. We put mother-tongue speakers in the driving seat of language development and cultural documentation.
